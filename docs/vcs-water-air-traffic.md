# Ambient boat traffic and AIRTRAIN

## How ambient boats approach a merge

In the US executable, `00431DC4` assigns boats spawned on waterways to physics status 3. Normal cruising uses mission 1 and driving style 0, updating navigation through `0019FDA0 → 004380E0 → 00438420 → 00438A80`. For boats with handling type 4, `004322C4–00432348` draws a seven-bit random number. A value below 40 selects style 2 and adds 10 to the cruise value. The global driving style override can also trigger this branch.

- **Route selection.** `004361A8` samples an adjacent edge, checks that it has a lane in the direction of travel, checks the turn class against the current lane, and applies node restrictions. If the first pass fails, it relaxes the turn requirement. If the next pass fails, it scans in adjacency order. `004369D0` classifies turns from the cross product of unit directions using thresholds of ±0.77. If the next segment has a planar length of at least 14, there is a 1/4 chance of shifting the previous lane index by one in either direction before clamping to the new lane count. Boats can select the same lane where a channel narrows.
- **Detecting a boat ahead.** Style 0 follows `00433AE8 → 00434948 → 00434B68`. Other collidable vehicles qualify if their collision sphere centers differ from the boat's by less than 16 on each horizontal axis and less than 5 vertically. The dot product of the planar displacement between the entity origins and the boat's actual forward direction then selects the forward half-plane. Style 2 skips this scan.
- **Slowing down.** `00434F20` uses relative motion to sweep the four corners of one hull against the other hull's oriented rectangle, then repeats with the roles reversed. For the earlier contact parameter `t`, the candidate speed limit is the cruise value multiplied by `max(0, (t − 0.2) / 1.3)`. The lowest candidate limit wins. The routine returns 1 when there is no intersection, so a nearby vehicle ahead but clear of the predicted track can still limit the boat to 8/13 of cruise. Its restriction clears when it leaves the selection area.

MTA approximates the PS2 collision sphere center with the entity origin and samples at the client's rail update rate. Linear velocity moves the boat along its route. The bow turns through the shortest angle toward the actual direction of travel, with a maximum correction of 90° per second. This rate is an MTA adaptation. Steering preserves angular velocity. Both styles retain MTA's predictive braking on curved paths for diagonal merges. The PS2 also has a 15-second head-on deadlock branch that considers both autopilot timers, opposing headings, and entity address order. That branch remains outside the merge implementation.

The automated regression uses two moving hulls to test crossing paths and a diagonal merge into the same lane. At each step it checks separation, braking, and recovery after the speed limit clears. The test runs the production Lua controller, advances hull positions in the harness, and rejects NPC reads or writes of angular velocity.

## Testing in MTA

- **Waterway spawning.** Observe waterway node 1163 outdoors in dimension 0, near `(-257, 1016, 6)`. Use `/trafficdebug boat` to inspect navigable directions. Track the same boat, driver seat, and bow heading through a bend and into the next segment. The server reports the managed boat count in `getTrafficRoadStatus().boats`. To isolate water traffic, set cars density to 0 and leave boats at 100%.
- **Airport and high-altitude flight.** Route point 0 is at `(-1684, -1023, 14)` and point 126 at `(-1549, 87, 450)`. These are route points. The clock determines the aircraft's current position. Use `/trafficdebug plane` to inspect each slot and distinguish an aircraft yet to arrive from one streamed in nearby or rendered at a distance. On two clients, compare the same slot's phase, turn direction, and number of visible fuselages during the switch between near and distant rendering.
- **Normal startup.** Let traffic resolve original model 280 through the production model resource. Check all three slots, then test explicit binding and unbinding separately. Manually binding 577 bypasses automatic resolution and tests only the binding interface.
- **Jet ski model and handling.** Observe the waterway junction near `(-315, 908, 6)`. The server tags an ambient jet ski with original model 233 and uses MTA 473 as the hull. The client requests a separate model ID for JETSKI. Rendered components include `handlebars` and `boat_hi`. Test boarding the driver seat, accelerating into turns, dismounting, and boarding again. Check when the NPC releases control. Inspect spray both at rest and underway.

In that case the client used the original high-detail model, including both sets of landing gear. Cabin windows and engines were visible. Reproduce this case with a test camera far from the player.
- After approaching the helm of a naturally spawned MARQUIS sailboat (248), the player took the driver seat using `/boardboat`. Five seconds later the client still reported the player as driver with task `TASK_SIMPLE_CAR_DRIVE`. Use the bound enter-vehicle key, enter `boardboat` in the F8 console, or type `/boardboat` in chat.

## Special vehicle controllers

`[model]/vcs_vehicle` can start independently. `CVehicle` selects a Lua class by original model ID. `CBoat` handles helm reach and waterway steering. `CJetSki` inherits the boat controller and implements two-axis balance, planing, handlebars, and rider pose. `CPlane` inherits from `CAutomobile` and handles AIRTRAIN route sampling, attitude, and near and distant rendering. Traffic selects spawn points, manages driver ownership, and maintains three flight slots. SkyGfx draws wakes and spray.

On the server, call `bindVcsVehicle(vehicle, originalId)` for a vehicle created by another script, or use the following interface to create an original model through a running `vcs_model` resource. Externally created hulls regain their original vehicle class after the controller restarts. Factory-created vehicles are cleaned up when their creator or controller stops.

```lua
local vehicles = exports.vcs_vehicle
local ski = vehicles:createVcsVehicle(233, x, y, z)
if ski then
    outputDebugString(vehicles:getVcsVehicleClass(ski))  -- CJetSki
end
```

- The waterway planner calls `steerBoatTraffic` with the current direction, look-ahead tangent, speed, and vertical velocity. The server manages flights with `setPlaneRoutes`, `samplePlaneRoute`, and `updatePlaneRouteVehicle`. Clients use `setPlanePresentationRoute`, `setPlanePresentationSlot`, `setPlanePresentationState`, and `getPlanePresentationStatus`. Each calling resource owns its own routes and presentation slots.
- The local VCSPC checkout declares `CVehicle → CAutomobile → CPlane` in `VCS PC/Vehicle.h`. Its `handlingIDs.h` and `VCSPC.cpp` contain the JETSKI ID and name. No standalone CJetSki implementation was found in that checkout. The Lua subclass represents PS2 CBoat subtype 2. The PC Edition installation's `data/handling.dat` supplies the corresponding vehicle and water handling parameters.
- In the US `0019F728`, the brake filter coefficient is 0.1, the dead zone is 0.05, and reverse throttle is −0.3 times the brake input. Rudder angle is the signed square of the steering input multiplied by 40°. When the player takes the seat, the controller disables native thrust on 473. CJetSki applies force using nozzle immersion, rudder angle, and throttle. Original runtime acceleration is 0.000256 and hull turn mass is 1000.01465.
- `00369630`, `00369BD8`, `00369EE8`, and `0036A090` use the original hull collision box and nine-point buoyancy table. `0019FDA0`, `001A3150`, and `001A3650` handle planing and jet thrust, center-of-mass movement, and water resistance, respectively. The MTA client samples the water and runs the underlying physics solver. After native hull damping, the controller corrects horizontal velocity by the ratio of the two hulls' damping factors. It integrates vertical speed separately to keep lateral slip on a banked hull from applying vertical force a second time.
- `001DC3E8` uses the neutral, lateral, forward, and backward BIKES associations. The two pose axes use time-step exponential smoothing with factors 0.86 and 0.89. Taking the seat immediately ends the boarding transition and overrides the swimming pose. The rider uses the original bone curves and root displacement. The current skin's limb lengths align the palms with the rotated rubber grips and the feet with the footrests. The saddle surface is 0.152 metres above ped_frontseat. The MTA driver's pelvis has a further 0.08 metres of skin clearance. The grip center is 0.3252 metres laterally from the pivot. Ankle targets in hull-local coordinates are (±0.300, −0.500, 0.235).
- The US engine audio path `0019C200 → 001B5D68 → 001BD438` selects sample 16 from SET0 bank 0. The collision ejection path `001A5648 → 001A4C50 → 003F0A58` checks the directional impulse. Capsizing requires the hull up-vector's Z component to fall below −0.6 and buoyancy to exceed 0.0008.

Local MTA 1.7 tests on flat water covered skins 0 and 7, boarding from a swim, about five seconds at full throttle and full lock in each direction, collision ejection, and remounting. Driver bone positions were recorded relative to the hull. The midpoint between wrist and finger base was compared with the rotated grip center on the same frame. For both skins the maximum sampled steering error was below 1 millimetre at a coordinate output precision of 0.001 metres. In sustained turns the hull center had previously sunk about 0.90 metres below the surface. After removing the duplicate vertical coupling, the steady full-lock depth under the same 74 FPS conditions was about 0.25 metres. This is an MTA measurement. A matching frame-by-frame PS2 emulator trace for the same inputs is still unavailable.

### PS2 boat wakes and spray

- `0035AEB8` samples the wake at the hull center. `00359D08 → 0035A358` uses `waterwake`. The history holds up to 32 samples for a player driver and 16 for other drivers. The JETSKI wake starts at twice the usual half-width at the hull. Up to four wakes are rendered on the surface at once.
- The spray sheets on either side come from `003F29D8 → 00177AC8`. `00177C98` advances 16 vertex pairs per side. The waterline and airborne vertices decay separately. `00178038` binds `004874E8`, which resolves to the original `boatwake` texture.
- Spray combines particles `0x2C` and `0x2D`. The former uses `carsplash_01–04` with ONE/ONE blending. The latter uses `boatwake2` with SRCALPHA/ONE blending. Each hull's handling supplies the emission positions, velocities, and spread on both sides. For a player, spray on the outside of a turn varies with angular velocity. For NPCs, the positive-throttle stern jet also follows the alternating-frame emission branch at `001A2098`.
- Rebuild the six added textures with `python tools/vcs_boat_textures.py "<US disc directory>"`. SkyGfx loads them at resource startup and owns particle creation for jet skis and traffic boats.
