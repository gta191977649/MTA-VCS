# Civilian traffic response to a siren

## Original game behavior

The VCS comparison uses the US PS2 executable `SLUS_215.90` in Ghidra. The SA comparison uses `CCarAI::MakeWayForCarWithSiren` at `CarAI.cpp:389` in gta-reversed commit `04c5b6643a9059e879f6015ec02e618f141fb27a`. The local Windows SA executable has the 1.0 address layout and SHA-256 `f01a00ce950fa40ca1ed59df0e789848c6edcf6405456274965885d0929343ac`. The checkout's `CarCtrl.cpp:849` forwards the physics controller to native address `00437C20`.

| Situation | VCS PS2 | Checked SA executable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who emits the request? | The automobile and motorcycle calls at `0013EF68` and `0033C648` allow NPC or player drivers. | The native call at `006B2BED` matches `Automobile.cpp:880–886`. It requires the current player's automobile, an active siren, a siren-capable model other than Mr. Whoopee, and frame 5 of eight. |
| Eligible moving traffic | `0042B718` requires source horizontal speed ≥0.1, recipient speed >0.05, height difference <5, distance <20 + 45 × source speed and a recipient ahead of the source. | `0041D68B–0041D890` also requires speeds ≥0.1 and >0.05, measured as magnitudes, and height difference <5. Distance is <40 + 45 × source speed. Both executables skip a stationary recipient. |
| Traffic in the forward cone | When position alignment >0.8 and heading alignment >0.7, the sign of the lateral displacement selects action 9 on the right or 10 on the left. Both swerve away. | `0041D942–0041D9BD` uses the determinant `dx × sourceDirectionY − dy × sourceDirectionX`. Positive selects action 20, zero or negative selects 21. Oncoming traffic with heading alignment <−0.9 swaps these choices. The final action 21 becomes WAIT. Same-direction traffic on the right therefore pulls right, while traffic on the left waits. Both receive 2,500 ms. Only active actions 20/21 suppress reassignment, so repeated aligned observations can extend the direct wait. |
| Crossing traffic approaching the source position | Other eligible moving traffic ahead waits for 2,000 ms if its velocity points toward the source position. | An approaching vehicle inside the cone with intermediate heading waits 4,000 ms. An approaching vehicle outside the cone waits 2,000 ms, including in the rear half-plane. Both branches preserve an active WAIT (1) or BRAKE (24). |
| Steering and stopping | `004380E0` applies zero gas, 0.001 brake and ±0.15 radians steering for a 2,000 ms swerve. It reverses the steering during the final 1,000 ms and clears the action at expiry. | `0043803E–004380B3` writes −0.25 radians for action 20 (physically right) or +0.25 for 21, zero gas, 0.001 brake and no handbrake. It countersteers during the final 1,250 ms and enters WAIT for another 4,000 ms after expiry. WAIT at `00437DD4` uses straight steering, zero gas and 0.2 brake without the handbrake. Later scans can supersede the wait. |

Three details differ from the SA C++ transcription. The executable checks source speed and takes the square root of the recipient's speed squared, selects the side with a subtraction at `0041D960`, and protects WAIT/BRAKE in the four-second crossing branch at `0041D910`. The C++ uses a squared recipient speed threshold, a dot product for side selection, and a different crossing-action guard.

## November 21 VCS-style live run

A real MTA client connected to an isolated loopback server on November 21, 2026. The fixture staged a VCPD Cruiser (VCS model 207) and applied ordinary accelerator input. The traffic resource spawned, routed and drove each civilian recipient. After each response expired, the fixture moved the cruiser clear and measured the civilian's subsequent travel. Distances are horizontal game units. Speeds are horizontal MTA velocity magnitudes.

| Approach | Measured result |
| --- | --- |
| Player cruiser following, siren off | Four samples met the moving-source, range, heading and forward-cone checks. The civilian continued without a siren response. At the last sample the separation was 16.26 and cruiser speed was 0.192. |
| Same approach, siren switched on | At separation 16.39, the cruiser and model 177 civilian were moving at 0.251 and 0.180. The synchronized NPC applied 0.286 right steering at 51 ms, then 0.286 left at 1,213 ms. Its rightward offset from the original heading increased from 0.26 to 0.88. The response cleared at 2,002 ms. It subsequently traveled 4.27 units within 11.64 s and reached speed 0.179. [Player cruiser and yielding civilian](siren-civilian-right.jpg) |
| Player cruiser moving away, siren on | Eight samples placed the moving civilian behind the moving cruiser and within range. No response appeared. The final forward projection was −16.50 at separation 16.59. |
| Player cruiser approaching opposing traffic | The model 201 civilian received `wait` at separation 14.56, with source speed 0.112 and civilian speed 0.140. Client samples showed zero horizontal velocity from 324 through 1,878 ms. The state cleared at 2,048 ms. Within another 1.72 s, it traveled 3.24 units and reached speed 0.083. [Oncoming civilian and player cruiser](siren-civilian-wait.jpg) |
| NPC-driven cruiser following, siren on | The fixture put an NPC in the driver seat with the player as passenger. Within another 15.02 s it traveled 2.09 units and reached speed 0.099. [NPC cruiser and yielding civilian](siren-npc-cruiser-left.jpg) |

The connected client streamed and synchronized each recipient and agreed with the server on the active mode. The cruiser emitted native world-sound group 17, index 10 with the siren enabled in each positive case. Five default-output loopback samples during the player's swerve measured mixed-output RMS of 0.111–0.145. This is a measurement of the full game mix. The source-linked native event identifies the cruiser.

All linked frames are original 960 × 600 client captures accepted by the server and visually reviewed. The client script log recorded no errors during the runs. The fixture logged a warning when its optional scenario selector file was absent and ran all scenarios from the beginning. In one opposing-traffic setup the original recipient left before activation, so the fixture selected another moving civilian. The fixture released driving input and camera control, and the isolated server was stopped after verifying its executable path and process ID.
