# Pursuit escalation, moving ram and block

An isolated loopback server and a real MTA 1.7 client ran these scenes on 21 November 2026. A temporary resource observed the traffic roster, client control owner, assigned task, actual vehicle collisions and player inputs. Production traffic selected the marked patrol cars, seeds, routes and missions. For the two targeted maneuvers the fixture repositioned the player's SUV on the approach, then drove it with the accelerator. The patrol received all its inputs from the production AI.

## Two-junction pursuit under red signals

The production roster assigned a moving marked cruiser and its driver to the player. The fixture placed that unit once on the 389→342 approach facing east and parked the occupied SUV at (−761, 203). It left subsequent steering, speed, route selection and mission transitions to the traffic resources. The client held signal state 2 through both junctions.

Before the lane geometry correction, the incoming lane at the second right turn ended within a few units of the next link's midpoint. One run overshot the turn and stopped 21 units from the player. The revised route intersects the actual offset lane centrelines and puts the next waypoint ahead on the exit lane. A Lua regression exercises this combination of a wide approach and a short exit link.

On the final client run, the same model 207 (unit 0x1B9, driver 0x1BA) reached the first junction on block mission 4. The wanted level rose from two to three stars while the car retained seed 1 and increased its cruise command from 34 to 43. Its heading at the first junction sample was 217.0°, then 170.4° on the next approach. At the second light it turned onto the target street, reached a heading of 121.8°, entered close block mission 5 at a sampled 18 units and stopped at ten units. The driver remained in seat zero at the destination gate.
